# Design direction for the artefacts

The five playbook viewers (`graph`, `value-map`, `ai-exposure`, `reshuffle`, `world-model`), the structure pages, and the README have to share a visual language. This document is the brief.

The artefacts are not SaaS dashboards. They are not internal reports. They are **editorial pieces about a specific organization** — long-form, restrained, dense, rewarding close reading. Closer to *La Lettura* (Corriere della Sera) and Giorgia Lupi's annotated diagrams than to Datadog.

## Designers we steal from

### Giorgia Lupi — humanist data viz

Reference: *Dear Data* (with Stefanie Posavec), the New York Times "Bruises" piece, La Lettura covers, the *Coronavirus Diaries*, *Data Items* MoMA exhibit.

What to steal:

- **Custom geometric marks per data type** instead of generic dots — squares for one kind, hollow circles for another, segmented rings for time, hatching for state. Each artefact develops its own little alphabet, explained in a key.
- **Marginalia** — small annotations next to a specific data point with a thin line connecting the two. The annotation is part of the composition, not a tooltip on hover.
- **Density without clutter** — pack a lot of information in a small space, but use hierarchy (size, weight, position) so the eye chooses what to read first.
- **Labels integrated with marks** — text rotated to follow a stroke, embedded inside a band, treated as a typographic ornament.
- **Hand-warmth in rendered output** — slight irregularity, organic curves, paper-feel. The opposite of "perfect grid".

### Accurat (Lupi, Bennett, Ciuccarelli) — Italianate editorial diagrams

Reference: their *La Lettura* covers (especially 2013–2017), *Brain Drain*, *Nobels no Degrees*, *La Repubblica* graphics.

What to steal:

- **Italianate masthead** at the top of every artefact: small italic kicker (`graph · for outline & co. · 2026-05-09`) above a tight-letter-spacing display title above a one-paragraph lede in larger body text.
- **Numbered annotations**: `01.` `02.` `03.` next to data items, with the number set in a contrasting weight or italic. Treat numbering as ornament, not list-bullet.
- **Disclosed grid**: the underlying column structure is faintly visible (vertical hairlines every N pixels). Builds editorial trust.
- **Small multiples**: the same chart shape repeated across a row to compare across categories. We use this in the screenshot kit for the README.
- **Mixed typographic register**: regular body + UPPERCASE LETTER-SPACED EYEBROWS + *italic asides* + **bold figures**. Each register does specific work.
- **Footer colophon**: a small italic block at the bottom with provenance ("Built from N citations across M sources, generated by skills/playbooks/<name> on <date>"). Magazine-grade.

### Federica Fragapane — narrative geometric structures

Reference: *La Lettura*, *Calvino's Cities*, *The Stories Behind a Line*.

What to steal:

- **Tree/branch metaphors** — when one node decomposes into many, render the decomposition as branches with thickness encoding weight. We can use this in `world-model` for the capability stack and in `value-map` for the activity ladder.
- **Color encodes one dimension, shape another, position a third** — strict separation of channels so the viewer can read each axis cleanly.
- **Text following a path** — labels that bend along curves rather than sitting on horizontal baselines. Use sparingly; in our case maybe along the bezier edges in `graph`.

### Stefanie Posavec — material data

Reference: *Dear Data*, *I Am a Book. I Am a Portal to the Universe*, *Writing Without Words* (visualization of *Madame Bovary*).

What to steal:

- **Data as material** — circles aren't dots, they're physical things with weight and texture. Use SVG filters (drop-shadow, subtle noise) sparingly to make marks feel like ink on paper.
- **Legends as part of the page** — never floating chips at the top, always integrated into the editorial layout with prose explaining what each mark means.

### Nadieh Bremer — Visual Cinnamon

Reference: *Why Are We Still Awake?*, *Inside Music* (Google Arts & Culture), her D3 generative work.

What to steal:

- **Custom color systems** — never the D3 default categorical scale. Tinted, saturated, harmonic.
- **Glow and emphasis** — used purposefully to direct attention. We use this in `graph` when a node is focused.
- **Generative ornament** as composition — small repeated motifs that arrange around the data without distracting.

### Density Design (Politecnico di Milano)

Reference: their bachelor-thesis books, *Final Synthesis Studio* outputs.

What to steal:

- **Print-feel even on screen** — generous margins, paginated layout cues, page numbers, drop caps.
- **Diagrams as objects** — each chart has a frame, a title, a caption, a credit line. Treated like a figure in a book.

### Mike Bostock / Observable — exemplar D3 work

Reference: NYT election graphics 2008–2014, his Observable notebooks, the official D3 gallery.

What to steal:

- **Smart defaults from data** — color domains, axis ticks, label positions all derived from the data, never hardcoded.
- **Annotations integrated** — `d3-annotation` style: line + label + connector calibrated per chart.

### NYT Graphics Desk

Reference: virtually anything by Gregor Aisch, Amanda Cox, Yau, Quealy. The Pudding for narrative.

What to steal:

- **Editorial captions** under every chart that say "what to read here". Not redundant titles — actual reading guidance.
- **Mobile-first responsive logic** — same chart adapts gracefully. We have this partially via SVG `preserveAspectRatio`.

## Concrete rules we adopt

These are the rules that go into `skills/design.py` v4 and that every viewer must follow.

### 1. Italianate masthead on every artefact

Top of each viewer:

```
[KICKER · italic small caps · letter-spaced]
[Display title · tight letter-spacing · weight 700 · max 5rem]
[One-paragraph lede in 18px body]
[Dateline · italic small · the date the play was generated]
[Tag chips · the structural tags relevant to this play]
```

### 2. Numbered sections with editorial register

Section headers use `0.1 ─ Section title` instead of `## Section title`. The number is in a contrasting weight (italic 200) and the rule character `─` is part of the typography.

### 3. Disclosed grid

Faint vertical hairlines every 20.6% of container width (5-column grid) visible at low opacity. They show through hero blocks but disappear under dense charts. This is the "paper rule" of the artefact.

### 4. Custom marks per data kind

Each node kind has a custom geometric mark, not a generic dot:

- **unit** — square (◼) — solid territories
- **activity** — circle (●) — operational primitives
- **person** — circle with inner dot (⊙) — humans-in-loop
- **role** — square outline (▢) — empty containers
- **stakeholder** — triangle (▲) — directional, "served by"
- **commitment** — diamond (◆) — bind, structural force
- **financial-summary** — square with cross (⊞) — accounting
- **identity** — six-point star — declarations
- **language-term** — small dash (━) — glossary
- **source** — open square (▢) hollow — referenced documents

The marks are rendered as composite SVG, not unicode. Keys explain them in the legend prose, not as a chip row.

### 5. Color scales 5-stop

Each data-viz hue has 5 stops: `--ds-{kind}-50/200/400/600/900` plus `--ds-{kind}-glow`. Used as:

- `-50` — background tint (very faint)
- `-200` — secondary fills
- `-400` — primary fills (today's flat token)
- `-600` — strong accents
- `-900` — text on light bg, ornaments
- `-glow` — drop-shadow glow on focus/hover

Brand surfaces stay monochrome. Scales live in viewers only.

### 6. Annotation marginalia

The graph viewer's side panel becomes editorial: instead of "Outgoing (5) / Incoming (3)" raw lists, it reads as a curated commentary about the focused node. Lupi-style asides connecting the main figure to small annotations.

### 7. Editorial footer / colophon

Bottom of each viewer:

```
Built from <N> citations across <M> sources.
Generated by skills/playbooks/<name>/build.py on <date>.
Audit gate: pass · Autoresearch: <dimensions> pass.
```

Italic, small, right-aligned.

### 8. Hand-warmth via stroke variation

Edges in the graph use a subtle stroke gradient (slightly thicker at one end) and a 1-pixel offset on bezier control points. Not random — calibrated. Reads as drawn-with-intent, not perfectly machined.

### 9. Material via shadow + paper texture

Cards / panels use `--surf-paper` (very subtle off-white background + faint grain texture via SVG filter or background-image dataurl). `--surf-inset` is the dark canvas variant for the graph. `--surf-raised` for hover-elevated cards.

### 10. Motion: cascading entry, restrained hover

Entry choreography: when a viewer first paints, elements fade in with a tiny stagger (50ms between siblings). Hover is fast (180ms) but considered (cubic-bezier easing).

## What we explicitly don't do

- Glassmorphism (overused, dates badly)
- Neon (clashes with editorial)
- Material Icons (we draw our own marks)
- Drop shadows on body text (we draw light filters on data marks only)
- 3D / parallax (the artefact is a page, not a stage)
- Mode switching (light/dark toggles) — each viewer has one canonical mode chosen for the data; the graph is dark inset, the others editorial white

## Application order

1. `skills/design.py` v4 — tokens. Backward-compatible (existing tokens stay).
2. Masthead pattern → applied to all 5 viewers.
3. Per-viewer Lupi/Accurat polish (one signature move per viewer).
4. Screenshot kit for README.

After this pass, every artefact opens with a recognizable Playable Org masthead and closes with the colophon. The middle (the actual chart / map / graph) stays each playbook's signature shape but with consistent typographic and material register.
