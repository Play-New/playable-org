# Outline & Co. — Role descriptions, 2025 edition

_Internal document maintained by the operations lead. Reviewed annually; this
edition reflects the studio at twenty-three people._

## Strategy

The strategy area carries five people: the lead and four mid-level
strategists. No juniors at this date.

The **strategy lead** runs every strategy engagement end-to-end. They scope
the engagement at pitch (working with client services), facilitate the
narrative workshop with the client (typically two half-days, on-site), write
the positioning document, run the audience research with one strategist, and
hand a written brief to the design lead. They also do the competitive audit
work for new pitches when the studio is in front of a prospect.

**Strategists** run audience research interviews, transcribe and synthesise,
write competitive audits, and develop naming systems on briefed projects.
Each strategist runs roughly four engagements per year, in parallel rather
than serial.

## Design

The design area carries seven people: the lead, three senior designers, two
mid-level designers, one junior designer.

The **design lead** runs every identity engagement end-to-end. They sit at
every kickoff workshop, define the visual language for the brief, supervise
identity-system construction, and reviews every deliverable before it leaves
the studio. They also build the brand book themselves — that's the artefact
the studio is best known for and the lead does not delegate it.

**Senior designers** run the identity-system build for individual
engagements: typography, colour, grid, motion logic, illustration, photo
direction. They typically own one engagement at a time.

**Mid-level designers** support the senior on identity-system build and own
the production of brand-book chapters under the lead's review. They
typically work across two engagements at a time.

The **junior designer** is on a structured development arc — paired with a
senior, paid for studio learning time, and currently producing studio
internal work (the studio website redesign, internal templates) plus
shadowing client work.

## Digital product

The product area carries five people: the lead, two senior product
designers, two mid-level product designers.

The **product lead** runs UI design, UX research, and design-system
construction for the product engagements. They have a stronger engineering
literacy than the design lead — they can read and review front-end code from
client teams and translate design decisions into engineering tickets.

**Senior product designers** own UI design for individual engagements
including prototyping, accessibility audits, and pairing with the client's
front-end developers. **Mid-level product designers** support seniors and
own design-system documentation when the client is building a system the
studio will hand off.

## Client services

The client services area carries three people: the lead and two
account/project managers.

The **client services lead** runs the kickoff workshop for every engagement
(scoping, calendar, weekly cadence agreement, single point of contact on the
client side), runs the weekly check-in calls, manages the asset handover
ritual at the end of each engagement, and runs scoping calls for new pitches
where they sit alongside the relevant discipline lead.

**Account managers** run the weekly check-ins on assigned engagements
(typically three each), manage asset handover logistics, and chase client
side approvals.

## Operations

The operations area carries three people: the lead and two operators.

The **operations lead** runs studio finance (P&L, vendor invoicing, client
invoicing), hiring (job specs, candidate funnels, structured interviews),
HR (employment contracts, quarterly reviews), and vendor management
(maintaining the studio's vendor partner list and the SLA conversations).

**Operators** run financial reconciliation, run the vendor invoice cycle
(every two weeks), maintain the hiring pipeline funnel, and prepare
materials for the quarterly studio finance review the founder runs.

## The founder / creative director

The founder runs the studio. They review any deliverable that combines two
or more disciplines, run the quarterly studio finance review, sit at every
enterprise-client kickoff, and personally lead the strategy + identity +
product flow for the largest engagement of any given quarter.

The founder does not own a client engagement portfolio. They do not run
weekly check-ins. They do not produce deliverables on engagements that
belong to a discipline lead.

## Governance

The four leads (strategy, design, product, client services) plus the
operations lead and the founder meet on the first Monday of each month for
the studio review. Decisions about new disciplines, new client tiers, new
offices, headcount changes above the cap, and major vendor partner
relationships are made in this meeting by consensus of the discipline
leads + the founder. Operations runs the meeting agenda and keeps the
notes.
